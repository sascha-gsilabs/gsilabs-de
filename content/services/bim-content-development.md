    eyebrow: What we build
    title: >-
      When an engineer drags your product into their model, the specification is
      effectively decided. Everything before that is trying to get into the model.
    lede: >-
      A PDF catalogue asks a specifier to do the modelling work themselves. Most will
      reach for whatever is already available as an object instead, and that decision
      sticks for the rest of the project. Getting your range into that library, in the
      formats the design tools expect and at the level of detail they can carry, is what
      this work is.
    items:
      - title: Product Families and Objects
        body: >-
          Native content for Revit, Archicad, Allplan and Tekla, parametric where your
          range is parametric, carrying the technical data a specifier needs rather than
          only the geometry.
      - title: Configurators
        body: >-
          Interactive tools that let an engineer size and specify a product visually and
          take away the object and the documentation, without waiting for a sales call.
      - title: Data That Stays Right
        body: >-
          Content is only useful while it matches the catalogue. We build so updates flow
          from your product data rather than being remodelled by hand every time a range
          changes.
    note: >-
      * Manufacturers have reported a marked drop in repetitive technical enquiries once
      the data is in the object. What a given range achieves depends on the market and
      how specifiers there work.

  - type: stages
    id: how
    title: How we work on it
    items:
      - title: Work Out What the Model Needs
        body: >-
          We go through your range with your technical team and establish what an object
          actually has to carry. Which parameters does a specifier set, which are fixed,
          and which properties have to survive into a schedule or a tender document. Too
          much detail makes a model unusable, too little makes the object worthless. This
          step decides which it is.
      - title: Build and Test in the Authoring Tools
        body: >-
          Content is produced for each target platform and tested the way it will be
          used, in real project files, at real project sizes. We check that it places
          correctly, schedules correctly, and does not drag a model to a halt when
          somebody puts two hundred of them in a facade.
      - title: Publish and Keep Current
        body: >-
          The content goes out through the channels your customers use, your own site and
          the libraries they search. From there we keep it aligned with your catalogue as
          products change, so what a specifier downloads is what you actually sell.

  - type: statement
    title: Content that is wrong is worse than content that is missing.
    body: >-
      A specifier who finds an object that does not schedule properly, or whose data does
      not match the technical sheet, will not come back for the next project. We treat the
      content as part of the product: checked against your own documentation, tested in
      real models, and maintained as the range moves.
    points:
      - Built for each authoring tool natively, not converted from one master file
      - Technical data taken from your documentation, so the object and the sheet agree
      - Maintained against your catalogue, so published content does not drift out of date
